01 — Features

Accurate by design. Careful by default.

Built for big photo libraries, messy Downloads folders and old backups.

01

By content.

Every candidate is fingerprinted with BLAKE3 or SHA-256, so renamed, moved and re-downloaded copies are all found, with an optional byte-by-byte check.

  • Whole duplicate folders, like a backup of a backup
  • Rescans read only what changed
02

By likeness.

Photos that look the same after being resized, re-saved or recompressed, and songs or videos that sound the same in another format or bitrate.

  • Picks the best shot of a burst
  • Spots blurry, dark and screenshot images
03

Safely.

The last copy of anything is never removed. Files are checked again right before removal, and anything changed since the scan is skipped.

  • Recycle Bin / Trash or DigiClean's Recovery Bin
  • Or replace copies with hard links

05 — Questions

Before you start.

Is it safe to remove what DigiClean finds?

Exact duplicates are grouped only when their contents are byte-identical. At least one copy in every group is always kept, each file is checked again right before removal, and anything that changed since the scan is skipped. By default removed items go to the Recycle Bin / Trash, so you can restore them.

Does DigiClean upload my files or collect data?

No. Scanning and fingerprinting happen entirely on your computer. DigiClean needs no account and makes no network connections, so no file names, fingerprints or usage data leave your device. See the privacy policy.

Windows shows “Windows protected your PC”

SmartScreen shows this for apps it hasn't seen often yet. Click More info, then Run anyway. You can check the download against the published SHA-256 checksums first.

macOS says the app can't be opened

If the build isn't notarized by Apple yet, open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway next to DigiClean (on older macOS: right-click the app and choose Open).

How do I run the Linux AppImage?

Make it executable and start it: chmod +x DigiClean-Linux-x86_64.AppImage, then ./DigiClean-Linux-x86_64.AppImage. On Debian or Ubuntu you can install the package instead: sudo apt install ./digiclean_amd64.deb.

How does it decide two files are duplicates?

Files are grouped by size, then by a quick fingerprint of sampled blocks, then by a full BLAKE3 (default) or SHA-256 hash of the complete contents. You can add a byte-by-byte comparison. Hard links are recognised as one file, so they are never counted as wasted space.

Get your disk back.

Scan a folder, see how much space the copies take, and decide what goes. It takes minutes, and nothing is removed until you say so.
