Matches by content

Every candidate is fingerprinted with BLAKE3 or SHA-256. Renamed, moved and re-downloaded copies are all found. Optional byte-by-byte check for absolute certainty.

Duplicate folders too

Finds whole folders with identical structure and contents, such as a backup of a backup, plus empty folders left behind.

Fast on huge drives

A parallel engine reads only what it must: files with unique sizes are skipped and fingerprints are remembered, so the next scan only reads what changed.

Safe cleanup

The last copy of anything is never removed, files are re-checked right before removal, and anything changed since the scan is skipped.

Smart select

Keep the newest, the oldest, or whatever is in your Pictures folder, across thousands of groups in one click. Fine-tune any group by hand.

Undo and recovery

Removed items go to the Recycle Bin / Trash or DigiClean's own recovery bin, ready to restore. You can also move duplicates or replace them with hard links.

Questions

Is it safe to remove what DigiClean finds?

Only files with byte-identical contents are grouped. In every group at least one copy is always kept, each file is checked again right before removal, and anything that changed since the scan is skipped. By default removed items go to the Recycle Bin / Trash, so you can restore them.

Does DigiClean upload my files or collect data?

No. Scanning and hashing happen entirely on your computer. DigiClean needs no account and sends no file names, hashes or usage data anywhere.

Windows shows “Windows protected your PC”

SmartScreen shows this for apps it hasn't seen often yet. Click More info, then Run anyway. You can check the download against the published SHA-256 checksums first.

macOS says the app can't be opened

If the build isn't notarized by Apple yet, open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway next to DigiClean (on older macOS: right-click the app and choose Open). macOS may also ask before DigiClean reads folders like Desktop or Documents; allow it to scan them.

How do I run the Linux AppImage?

Make it executable and start it: chmod +x DigiClean-Linux-x86_64.AppImage then ./DigiClean-Linux-x86_64.AppImage. On Debian or Ubuntu you can install the .deb instead: sudo apt install ./digiclean_amd64.deb.

How does it decide two files are duplicates?

Files are first grouped by size, then by a quick fingerprint of sampled blocks, then by a full BLAKE3 (default) or SHA-256 hash of the complete contents. You can add a byte-by-byte comparison. Hard links are recognised as one file, so they are never counted as wasted space.
